# REIblast Support Bot Knowledge Base: Master List

# Onboarding

## 1-1 Onboarding Form

Q: What is the onboarding form for and why does it matter so much?
A: The information you enter on the onboarding form is what REIblast uses to register you with the carriers so you can send text messages. If it doesn't exactly match your official records, your registration can be denied, which can cost you weeks. Take your time and enter everything precisely.

Q: What do I need in front of me before I fill out the onboarding form?
A: If you're registered with the IRS and have an EIN (whether you're an LLC or a sole proprietor), have your EIN letter from the IRS in hand. Everything you enter (business name, EIN, address) needs to match that letter exactly. There's also a "Before You Start" guide with tips on what to enter in each field.

Q: How should I enter my legal business name?
A: Enter it exactly as it appears on your IRS EIN letter (your CP575), character for character. If your letter says "Smith Holdings LLC," then "Smith Holdings" without the LLC gets denied, and if there are no periods in the name on your letter, there can't be any periods in your registration.

Q: I'm a sole proprietor and don't have an EIN. What do I put in the EIN field?
A: If you're a sole proprietor without an EIN, enter all ones in the standard EIN format: 11-1111111.

Q: I'm a sole proprietor but I do have an EIN. Do I still enter it?
A: Yes. If you have an EIN as a sole proprietor, enter the EIN shown on your EIN letter.

Q: Which business type should I pick?
A: Choose the type that matches your official records: LLC, corporation, sole proprietorship, or partnership. Most members are either an LLC or a sole proprietorship.

Q: Does my business address have to match anything specific?
A: Yes. Enter your business address exactly as it appears on your EIN letter. This address is used to submit your registration, and a mismatch with your IRS records is a common source of delays.

Q: Why can't I change the email at the top of the onboarding form?
A: That email is filled in automatically from the email you used at checkout and can't be edited on the form.

Q: Do I need a website to fill out the onboarding form?
A: No. The website URL field is optional, so you can leave it blank if you don't have one. REIblast gets every member a website whether or not you already have one, which keeps your A2P registration consistent.

Q: What do I enter for target market?
A: Enter the market you want to send your texts to, for example a city like Austin, Texas.

Q: What am I agreeing to on the consent screen?
A: Because REIblast registers your A2P on your behalf, you give your consent so that registration can be submitted for you, and REIblast handles the use-case description itself. You're agreeing that you'll only text property owners about purchasing their property, that you'll honor all stop/opt-out requests immediately and maintain a do-not-contact list, and that misuse of the REIblast SMS system may result in immediate account suspension.

Q: Can I get my account suspended for how I text?
A: Yes. Part of the onboarding consent is acknowledging that misuse of the REIblast SMS system may result in immediate account suspension. You also agree to honor opt-out requests immediately and keep a do-not-contact list.

Q: I made a mistake on my onboarding form. Can I fix it before submitting?
A: Yes. Before you submit, there's a review screen showing everything you entered. If anything is wrong, click Edit next to it to go back and change it, then continue through to submit.

Q: I closed the onboarding form before finishing it. How do I get back to it?
A: You'll receive a link to the form by email and by text message. Use that link to return and complete it.

Q: What happens after I submit the onboarding form?
A: The system verifies your information, and then you're prompted to book your onboarding call. Set your time zone correctly so the times display accurately, pick a time, enter your information, and schedule the meeting.

Q: What happens on the onboarding call?
A: You'll review the information you submitted to make sure everything is correct, go over your account, and learn your next steps toward getting approved and ready to start blasting.

## 1-2 Logging In

Q: Where do I go to log in to REIblast?
A: Go to app.reiblast.app, which is where you sign in to the CRM. If you forget that address, go to reiblast.app (the site you used to sign up) and click Log In, which takes you to the same login page. Bookmark app.reiblast.app so you always have it.

Q: I just signed up and was never given a password. How do I log in for the first time?
A: On your first login you only have your email, no password. On the login page, click Forgot Password, enter the email you used to set up your account, and click Reset Password. Open the reset email, click Reset Password, and set your new password, then sign in with your email and that password.

Q: I forgot my password. How do I reset it?
A: On the login page at app.reiblast.app, click Forgot Password, enter the email on your account, and click Reset Password. You'll receive a password reset link by email; click it and set a new password, then sign in.

Q: I requested a password reset but never got the email. What's wrong?
A: The most common cause is a typo or different email: if the email you enter doesn't match the one your account was set up with, a reset link will never be sent. If you've requested a reset and haven't received the link, contact support: text or call 915-800-8266 (text is preferred), email support@mail.reiblast.app, or message on Discord, and say you can't log in.

Q: It's asking me for a security code when I log in. Where does that come from?
A: After you enter your email and password, you're asked to have a security code sent to either your phone number or your email. Choose one, enter the code you receive, and you'll be taken into your account.

## 1-3 KYC Verification

Q: What is KYC verification and why do I have to do it?
A: KYC verification is a quick identity check. Until it's complete, you can't get a phone number and your A2P registration can't be submitted, because the submission process stops and requires verification to be finished first.

Q: Why can't I buy a phone number or start blasting?
A: If your KYC verification isn't complete, a phone number can't be purchased and your A2P registration can't be submitted. Once KYC is done, you still can't send until your A2P registration is approved. Finish KYC first in Settings, then Phone System, then Additional Settings, then KYC Verification.

Q: What do I need before I start KYC verification?
A: Have a valid, unexpired government-issued photo ID and your phone, since the facial scan is much easier to do on your phone. There's also a downloadable KYC quick step sheet that lists every step if you want to follow along.

Q: What kind of ID can I use for KYC?
A: You need an unexpired government-issued photo ID. The verification screen shows a list of accepted ID types to choose from, such as a driver's license.

Q: Where do I find KYC verification in my account?
A: From your dashboard, click Settings, then Phone System. In the row of tabs across the top of Phone System, scroll all the way to the right, click Additional Settings, then KYC Verification.

Q: How do I start KYC verification?
A: Go to Settings, then Phone System, then Additional Settings, then KYC Verification. If you haven't verified yet, you'll see a gray "Not Verified" status and a blue Complete Verification button. Click Complete Verification, click to begin verifying, select your country, and choose your ID type from the list.

Q: Can I do the KYC verification on my phone instead of my computer?
A: Yes. After you start verification on your computer, choose the option to continue on another device. You'll get a QR code to scan with your phone, and you can finish the process there.

Q: What does the KYC process actually involve?
A: First you take a picture of your ID, which must be an unexpired government ID with your face on it. Then you do a facial scan, similar to setting up Face ID on a new iPhone, to confirm your face matches your ID.

Q: My KYC says "Verification Pending." How long does it take?
A: Verification Pending means your identity check is being processed. It usually clears instantly and changes to a green Verified badge, but sometimes it can take a few days for your identity to be verified.

Q: How do I know my KYC verification went through?
A: In Settings, then Phone System, then Additional Settings, then KYC Verification, you'll see a green Verified badge and the blue Complete Verification button will be gone. That means you're verified.

Q: What do I do after KYC verification is done?
A: Once you're verified, the next step is your A2P registration, which is what allows you to start text blasting.

## 1-4-1 A2P Lesson 1

Q: What is A2P?
A: A2P stands for Application to Person. It's any text a business sends through software, which is exactly what REIblast does. Any account planning to text using a 10DLC number (a standard 10-digit U.S. number) has to register for A2P first.

Q: Why do I have to register before I can send texts?
A: The carriers (AT&T, T-Mobile, and Verizon) require it. They want to know who's texting their customers and how those people agreed to receive texts, as a way to keep spam off their networks. Until you're registered and approved, your messages get blocked or filtered and don't arrive.

Q: Why can't I start texting right away?
A: Any account texting from a 10DLC number has to be registered and approved by the carriers first. That approval doesn't come from REIblast or the platform, so you can't send until the carriers approve your registration.

Q: Who approves my A2P registration? Can REIblast speed it up?
A: Approval comes from the carriers through an organization called The Campaign Registry, not from REIblast or the platform. REIblast submits your registration for you, but the carriers make the decision on their own schedule.

Q: What can I do to get my A2P approved faster?
A: Finish your onboarding steps (the onboarding form, the onboarding call, and KYC verification) quickly so your registration can be submitted early. Make sure everything you entered matches your official records exactly, be patient while it's reviewed, and don't change your business details during review. Getting it right the first time is the fastest path, because a rejection restarts the clock.

Q: Can I change my business information while my A2P is being reviewed?
A: Don't. Changing your business details during review can cause a mismatch, and if your brand is rejected the clock restarts and your registration has to go through the regular processing times again.

## 1-4-2 A2P Lesson 2

Q: Do I have to file my A2P registration myself?
A: No. REIblast submits your A2P registration for you, including writing everything the carriers need to know about how you'll use texting. You don't need to learn the registration process.

Q: When does my A2P registration get submitted?
A: Your registration is submitted once three things are done: your onboarding form is complete, you've had your onboarding call, and you've completed KYC verification.

Q: What are the steps of A2P approval?
A: There are two review stages. First is brand review, which checks your business identity (legal name, EIN, and address). Once your brand is approved, your campaign (how you'll use texting) is submitted automatically and reviewed. After both are approved, each carrier updates on their end before you're fully live.

Q: How long does A2P approval take?
A: Plan on one to four weeks from submission to fully approved, since most of that time is waiting on the carriers. Approvals can come much faster, though: in as little as one to two business days for a sole proprietor, or two to three business days for an LLC.

Q: How long does brand review take?
A: Brand review is usually pretty quick and often clears the same day.

Q: How long does campaign review take?
A: Campaign review usually takes three to seven business days and can stretch toward two weeks when the carriers are busy.

Q: My brand and campaign are approved. Why am I still waiting?
A: After both are approved, every carrier has to update on their end, which can take a few more business days.

Q: I just got approved but some of my messages are failing. Is something wrong?
A: Some failures are normal right after approval, because the carriers don't all update at the same speed. They usually clear within a day, depending on whether the cause is a restriction or a suspension. If failures keep happening, reach out to support so they can take a look.

Q: How much does A2P registration cost?
A: Registration has a one-time fee of $23.50, which comes out of your account wallet.

Q: I'm on a free trial. Why was I charged by REIblast?
A: That's the one-time $23.50 A2P registration fee coming out of your account wallet while your registration goes through. It's expected even if you're on a free trial or promotion. You won't see other charges unless you start using paid features like calling, sending emails, or other usage.

## 1-4-3 A2P Lesson 3

Q: Why did my A2P registration get denied?
A: It's almost always a mismatch. The carriers compare the business information submitted for you against your official records, and if it doesn't match exactly (not close, exactly), they deny it. This usually happens when information was guessed instead of copied directly from the EIN letter.

Q: What kinds of mistakes get an A2P registration denied?
A: The most common mismatches are a legal business name that doesn't match what the IRS has, an EIN with even one wrong digit, an old address or one that doesn't match what's on file, and sole proprietors using a DBA or business nickname instead of their full legal name.

Q: What should I use as the source of truth for my business information?
A: If you have an EIN, your CP575 letter (your EIN letter from the IRS) is the source of truth, and your registration has to match it exactly as written. If you're a sole proprietor without an EIN, your source of truth is your legal identity on your ID plus the text verification you complete with The Campaign Registry.

Q: I've moved since I got my EIN. Which address do I use?
A: Use the address the IRS has on file for your EIN, not your new office address. That's the one the carriers check against.

Q: I put a different LLC name on my onboarding form than what's on my EIN letter. Is that a problem?
A: Yes, and it's the most common reason for a first rejection. The name you want to use may be different from the name on your EIN letter. REIblast makes sure all your business information matches your letter and resubmits, which usually fixes it.

Q: I'm a sole proprietor. What name will my business be registered under?
A: Sole proprietors are registered under the business name "[Your Last Name] Investments." For example, if your last name is Smith, your business is registered as Smith Investments.

Q: I got a text from The Campaign Registry asking me to verify. What do I do?
A: Reply YES. For sole proprietors, a verification text is sent to the authorized representative on your registration, and replying YES gives the authorization needed for your brand registration.

Q: My EIN is brand new and my registration got denied. What do I need to fix?
A: If your EIN was issued very recently, it may not show up in the verification system yet. That isn't your fault and there's nothing to fix; REIblast waits a little and resubmits.

Q: My A2P got denied. What happens now?
A: Don't stress. REIblast sees the specific reason for the denial, tells you exactly what needs to be corrected, and resubmits for you. A rejection does restart the clock, so once it's fixed your registration goes back through the regular processing times. Don't change your business details on your own during review, since that can cause another mismatch.

Q: Do I need to send REIblast my EIN letter?
A: If you're an LLC or have an EIN and are registered with the IRS, you'll be asked to send your CP575 letter (your IRS EIN letter) after your registration is submitted. That way it's on hand if your brand comes back rejected.

Q: My brand keeps getting rejected. What happens now?
A: If your brand is rejected three times in a row, REIblast sends your CP575 letter to GoHighLevel support, who review it and make the case to the carriers to approve your registration. REIblast handles that for you, and your campaign is then submitted manually.

Q: How do I contact REIblast support?
A: Text or call 915-800-8266 (text is preferred), email support@mail.reiblast.app, or message on Discord. If you can't log in, say so in your message. To book a support call, use the booking link in the Support Calendar channel in the Discord. A ticketing system inside the Discord is coming soon.

Q: Is there a guide I can reference for A2P registration?
A: Yes. There's an A2P Basics download in the course resources that walks through the essentials of getting your registration approved.

# Basics

## 2-1 Account Overview

Q: What am I looking at on my dashboard?
A: The dashboard is the overview of your entire account. It shows your total messages sent, how many replies you've gotten, how many motivated sellers you have, and how many deals you've had over the month. You can filter it by time and edit what it shows.

Q: Why did the numbers on my dashboard change?
A: The dashboard numbers are based on opportunity status. As you move opportunities through your pipeline, the dashboard updates to match.

Q: Can I schedule calls with sellers inside REIblast?
A: Yes. In Calendars you can set up a personal calendar and book appointments, such as a time to call a seller or a Zoom or Google Meet. You can also connect it to your Google Calendar.

Q: Who are these contacts already in my account?
A: Your account starts with example contacts. You can delete them from Contacts to start fresh before you import your own list.

Q: Where can I see where my leads are in the process?
A: In Opportunities, which is your pipeline. It shows where each lead stands, such as whether they've been texted, replied, are interested, or are under contract. Leads move through the pipeline stages automatically as they're messaged and respond.

Q: Where do my REIscore deal analyses show up?
A: If you push deals from REIscore into the CRM, they show up in Analyze Deals, which gives you an overview of everything you've pulled from REIscore. Note that REIscore is currently disabled.

Q: Do I need to set up payments?
A: Only if you plan to take payments from people in the future, in which case you can set up a payment gateway in Payments. Otherwise, the main thing you'll use in Payments is the contract templates.

Q: Do I need to use AI Studio or Memberships?
A: No. You don't need to worry about either one.

Q: Can I save quick replies so I don't have to type the same text every time?
A: Yes. In Marketing you can set up text snippets (also called canned messages) for quick replies, as well as email templates.

Q: Where's my website?
A: In Sites. Every REIblast account gets a generic template website that's filled in for you, and its main purpose is to help you get approved for A2P. You can also use Sites to build your own funnels, forms, and other websites.

Q: Can I edit or change my A2P website?
A: No. Do not touch the template website REIblast set up for you, because it's part of what keeps your A2P registration consistent. If you want your own site, funnel, or form, build a separate one in Sites.

Q: Where can I store photos, contracts, and other files?
A: In Media Storage. You can upload property photos, contracts, flyers, videos, and other files so they're easy to find. Think of it as Google Drive inside REIblast.

Q: How do I track what my VAs are doing?
A: Use Reporting. You can see each team member's numbers, like calls made and text messages sent, along with a range of other activity.

Q: How do I get into REItools, REIscore, or REIscrub?
A: Click REItools in the left sidebar to open the tools dashboard. You'll log in with a one-time password, which is texted to your personal phone from the REIblast support number. Every member has access to REIscore and REIscrub from day one, though REIscore is currently disabled.

Q: Where do I add users or buy phone numbers?
A: Both are in Settings, at the bottom of the left sidebar. That's also where you change any of your account settings.

Q: Is there a map of everything in my account?
A: Yes. There's a downloadable account map in the course resources that you can keep handy as a quick visual reference to everything in your account.

## 2-2-1 Payments

Q: Where can I see what plan I'm on and when I'll be billed next?
A: Go to Settings, then Billing. Billing has three sections: Subscriptions, Payments, and Wallet and Transactions. You'll land on Subscriptions, which shows your plan, how much it costs per month, and your next billing date.

Q: How do I cancel my subscription?
A: You can modify or cancel your plan from the Subscriptions page under Settings, then Billing. Before you cancel, message REIblast support and let them know what's going on so they can help get you taken care of.

Q: Where can I see my charges?
A: Go to Settings, then Billing, then Payments. All your charges show up there, including wallet recharges, usage charges, and subscription charges. Your subscription invoices are listed under Invoices.

Q: How do I update my card on file?
A: Go to Settings, then Billing, then Payments. Click the pencil icon next to your payment method and enter your new card.

Q: Why is my contact not in my workflow anymore?
A: If there was an error while sending to that contact, such as your wallet running out of funds or hitting your daily limit, the SMS error catch-all adds an SMS fail tag and removes the contact from the workflow. The contact keeps the tag for the message it was on, so when you add it back to the blast, it picks up where it left off, as long as you don't change its tags. It's a backup for emergencies, not something to rely on, so keep an active, funded card on file so your sends don't fail in the first place.

Q: Why was my account paused?
A: Your account gets paused for nonpayment if your subscription payment fails even once, or if a wallet auto recharge fails three times. Make sure you have an active, unlocked card with money on it linked to your account.

Q: What do I need to keep on my card so my account keeps running?
A: Keep an active card on file that's unlocked, has money on it, and has the correct card information. That card pays for your entire system, and if a payment fails, your texts fail. A single failed subscription payment, or three failed wallet recharges, will pause your account.

Q: Can I use my Cash App card for REIblast?
A: Yes. Just make sure there's money in your Cash App, the card is unlocked, and the card information on file is correct.

## 2-2-2 Wallet and Transactions

Q: What is my wallet?
A: Your wallet is a prepaid balance in dollars. Every text, call, email, and anything else that costs money inside REIblast draws from it, including the one-time A2P registration fee. When your balance runs low, auto recharge tops it up from your card on file.

Q: Where do I find my wallet?
A: Go to Settings, then Billing, then Wallet and Transactions.

Q: Did I spend more this month than last month?
A: Check the usage overview, which is the first thing you see in Wallet and Transactions. It compares this month to last month, shows the products you use the most, and includes a six-month chart.

Q: What am I spending the most money on?
A: The usage overview shows your top products by spend. For a full breakdown, open By Activity, which lists everything you can do in the system, how much you spent on each, and what percent of your bill it made up.

Q: Where do I see my A2P registration fee?
A: It shows up in By Activity, under Settings, then Billing, then Wallet and Transactions, alongside everything else that's been pulled from your wallet.

Q: How much money is in my wallet?
A: Open Wallet & Recharge in Wallet and Transactions. It shows your full balance breakdown: your wallet total, any credits, and your total balance. Your auto recharge settings and top-up history are there too.

Q: How can I see every charge to my account?
A: Open Transactions in Wallet and Transactions. Every charge gets its own row with a reference ID, the date it was billed, the activity date, a description, the amount, and a running total of your wallet balance.

Q: What are these carrier fees next to my texts?
A: The phone carriers charge a fee for every message you send. It's charged by the carriers, not by REIblast, and it's a normal part of business texting. Most of the time carrier fees don't appear in your transactions summary at all.

## 2-2-3 Wallet Settings

Q: What is auto recharge?
A: Auto recharge automatically adds funds to your wallet so your balance doesn't hit zero in the middle of a blast. It's on by default and can't be turned off; you can only change how it works.

Q: Can I turn off auto recharge?
A: No. Auto recharge is on by default, and the only thing you can change is how much it charges and when it triggers.

Q: Where do I change my auto recharge settings?
A: Go to Settings, then Billing, then Wallet and Transactions, and open Wallet & Recharge. The auto recharge section there controls how much is charged and when.

Q: What does the recharge amount mean?
A: The recharge amount is how much is charged to your card to refill your wallet each time your balance hits your low balance limit. It defaults to $10, and you can set it anywhere from $10 to $15,000. Most members keep it at $10.

Q: Should I set my recharge amount higher or lower?
A: Either is fine; set it to what's comfortable for you. A smaller amount like $10 means more frequent, smaller charges, while a larger amount like $50 means fewer, larger charges.

Q: What is the low balance limit?
A: The low balance limit is the balance that triggers an auto recharge. When your wallet falls below it, your recharge amount is charged. It defaults to $0, and you can set it anywhere from $0 to $1,000.

Q: What should I set my low balance limit to?
A: It's up to you. At $0, the system waits until your wallet is completely used up before it recharges. At something higher like $10, your wallet usually never drops below $10 before another recharge is charged.

Q: What are the best auto recharge settings?
A: The recommendation is to leave the defaults alone: a $10 recharge amount and a $0 low balance limit.

Q: Why does my wallet balance show negative?
A: Your wallet is prepaid, but it can go slightly negative while you're using the system. When it drops below your low balance limit, auto recharge kicks in to bring it back up.

Q: Why was I charged a little more than my recharge amount?
A: If your wallet went negative, the recharge covers that negative amount first and then adds your recharge amount. For example, at a balance of negative $0.45 with a $10 recharge amount, you're charged $10.45, which brings you back to zero and then adds $10.

## 2-2-4 Adding Funds Manually

Q: How do I add money to my wallet?
A: Go to Settings, then Billing, then Wallet and Transactions. Click Add next to your balance, enter the amount you want to add, and click Add. Your card is charged right away and the funds are added to your wallet.

Q: When should I add funds to my wallet manually?
A: Add funds before a big blast so none of it fails from low funds. Also add funds if you see that a recharge was attempted and didn't go through, for example because your card was locked.

Q: I added funds but my balance didn't change. What's wrong?
A: The new balance can take a moment to show up. Refresh the page to see it.

Q: Do I have to be on the Wallet & Recharge screen to add funds?
A: No. Your balance and the Add button appear on every view inside Wallet and Transactions, so you can add funds from any of them.

Q: Why would I need more money in my wallet as I go?
A: As you move up through the messaging limit levels, you're able to send more and more texts, which uses more wallet funds. Keep an eye on your balance and add funds when you're planning a bigger blast.

## 2-2-5 Putting It All Together

Q: How does billing work in REIblast?
A: Your card on file is the source of all the money in your account. It pays your subscription directly, and it funds your wallet through auto recharge or when you add funds manually. Your wallet then pays for usage, and every text, call, and email draws from it bit by bit. If anything in your billing ever looks off, check your card on file first.

Q: Does my wallet pay for my subscription?
A: No. Your subscription and your wallet don't share funds. Your subscription is charged directly to your card on file, and your wallet is funded from your card separately. Your wallet only pays for usage like texts, calls, and emails, so adding funds to your wallet won't cover your subscription.

Q: What happens if there's no money on my card?
A: Without funds on your card, your subscription can't be paid and your wallet can't be refilled. With no money in your wallet, you can't blast.

Q: Is there a cheat sheet for how billing and the wallet work?
A: Yes. There's a downloadable Wallet & Billing quick reference guide (PDF) in the course resources that covers your card on file, subscription, wallet, auto recharge, and transactions at a glance.

## 2-3 Text Blasting Rules

Q: Why do the texting rules matter so much?
A: The carriers that approved you to text also watch how you text. If they see spam patterns, like too many messages too fast, no way to opt out, or bulk texts at midnight, they start filtering your number. Once a number gets a bad reputation it's hard to fix, so following the rules keeps your number healthy.

Q: What are the rules for text blasting?
A: There are five: ramp up your volume gradually on a new number, give every message a way to opt out, respect quiet hours in the recipient's time zone, say who you are in the first line, and keep messages short (about 160 characters or less).

Q: Why can't I send all my texts on day one?
A: A brand-new number can't blast at full volume right away. It has daily limits that rise over time as it builds a track record, similar to building credit history before getting a higher limit on a new credit card.

Q: How many texts can I send per day?
A: Your daily messaging limit starts at 500 messages and moves up in tiers (for example to 750, then 1,000, then 1,500) up to 5,000+ messages per day. To move up to the next tier, you have to send the maximum number of texts allowed in your current tier. You can see your current limit in Settings, then Phone System, then Messaging, under the Messaging Limits tab.

Q: I hit my daily limit. When can I send again?
A: Once you hit your limit, you have to wait 24 hours for the next tier to unlock. The 24 hours starts from the very first message you sent that day, and you don't have to send all your messages at once, just within that 24-hour window.

Q: Do I have to put "STOP" in every text?
A: Every message needs a clear way to opt out, every time. It doesn't have to sound robotic (for example "Not selling, reply STOP" or "Wrong person, text STOP"), but it has to include an opt-out keyword so it's obvious how to opt out. When someone replies STOP, they're removed automatically and you don't text them again.

Q: What opt-out words can I use?
A: Your opt-out message can use STOP, STOP ALL, CANCEL, UNSUBSCRIBE, END, or QUIT.

Q: How do I change my opt-out message?
A: Go to Settings, then Phone System, then click Messaging and open Messaging Compliance. You can customize your opt-out message there, and it gets attached to the first message you send someone.

Q: Can I remove the opt-out language from my texts?
A: Keeping it in is strongly recommended because it's what the regulations require. If you still want it removed, contact REIblast support; it can only be turned off after you've been on the system for at least a month, and it requires going through additional consents.

Q: What hours am I allowed to send texts?
A: Only text during the daytime in the recipient's local time zone, not yours. The general window is 8 a.m. to 9 p.m., but some states are stricter, so the safest approach is to send between 9 a.m. and 8 p.m. their time and skip early Sunday mornings.

Q: I'm texting a list in a different time zone. Whose time do I go by?
A: The recipient's. If you're in Florida texting people in California, go by California time, and vice versa. Do the math before you hit send.

Q: What times do my built-in blasts run?
A: The built-in blasts are already set to follow quiet hours and run Monday through Friday from 9 a.m. to 6 p.m. in the recipient's time zone. If you want the times changed, contact REIblast support and they can update it for you.

Q: Do I need to say who I am in my texts?
A: Yes. People should know who's texting them from the very first line, because anonymous texts look like scams and get reported. Something as simple as "Hey John, this is Steven" works, and the built-in blasts already send an introductory message like this.

Q: How do I change the name my texts are signed with?
A: Go to Settings, then Phone System, then Messaging, then Messaging Compliance, and edit your signature (sender information). By default it signs with your sub-account name, like "Thanks, Test Company LLC," but you can change it to something more personal, like just your first name.

Q: Why was my text charged as more than one message?
A: Texts are charged per segment, not per message. A segment is about 160 characters, and anything longer gets split into multiple segments, each of which costs money and counts against your limits. A single emoji or special character can drop the segment limit to 70 characters, which can double or triple what a message costs.

Q: Can I use emojis in my texts?
A: You can, but avoid them. A single emoji or special character drops the segment limit from about 160 characters to 70, so one smiley face can double or triple what a message costs and count more against your limits.

Q: What does a good first text look like?
A: Something like: "Hey John, this is Steven, I drove past 123 Main Street the other day and I was wondering if you're the owner, not selling, reply stop, Steven." It says who's texting, asks one simple question, gives a way out with STOP, has no emojis, and fits in one segment.

Q: I'm writing my own blast. What do I need to watch out for?
A: The built-in blasts already follow the rules, but your own blasts need to as well: set the times they can run, include your intro and opt-out language, and keep them short. Merge fields (like name and address) change length for each contact, so leave room for them and keep the rest of the message tight to stay in one segment.

Q: Is there a cheat sheet for the texting rules?
A: Yes. There's a five-rules cheat sheet in the course resources for quick reference, along with a slide deck covering the texting rules.

## 2-4 Your Built-In Automations

Q: What automations come with my account?
A: Your account comes with two locked automation folders. The SMS Flow Campaign folder is the text blast machine that sends your blasts. The Internal Workflow folder runs everything behind the scenes that keeps your system working and compliant.

Q: Can I edit the built-in automations?
A: No. The two built-in folders are locked, and REIblast only updates them when something breaks or when an update goes out to all members. If you need changes, contact support. You're free to create your own automations, and they'll show up on the Automation page.

Q: What happens when a text goes to a bad or inactive number?
A: The messaging error fail-safe catches the most common SMS errors, like bad or inactive numbers. It adds a tag, turns on Do Not Disturb for calls and SMS on that contact, and removes the contact from all the workflows they're in.

Q: How do I stop a lead from getting any more automated texts?
A: Add the tag "Remove from All - Dead Lead" to the contact. That removes them from all automations so they don't receive any more text messages.

Q: What happens when a lead replies to my blast?
A: Any reply to one of the three preloaded SMS flows creates an opportunity named after the contact in the Lead Replied stage of the text blast pipeline. It also adds a "replied" tag and removes the lead from all workflows so they stop getting automated texts.

Q: Why did a contact get Do Not Disturb turned on automatically?
A: Every contact added to the CRM runs through number validation. If the number comes back as SMS incapable or not valid, such as a landline, Do Not Disturb for SMS is turned on automatically. Do Not Disturb is also turned on when a send hits a common error like a bad or inactive number.

Q: Why are there three SMS flows?
A: All three flows are structured the same way; only the wording changes. Splitting contacts across slightly different messages keeps it from looking like you're sending the exact same text to thousands of people.

Q: What texts does the blast send, and when?
A: Each flow sends seven texts spaced out over about 30 days: the intro message, then follow-ups after 1 day, 2 days, 3 days, 5 days, 5 days, and a week. The opt-out language is added to the end automatically. After the seventh message, if there's still no reply, the opportunity moves to No SMS Response.

Q: What does the first blast text say?
A: In Flow 1 it reads like: "Hey John, this is Steven. I drove past 123 Main Street the other day and I was wondering if you were the owner," with your opt-out language added to the end. The name, your name, and the address are merge fields filled in from each contact. Flows 2 and 3 use slightly different wording.

Q: Why are the follow-up texts spaced so far apart?
A: Text blasting means sending to many people at once, not sending a bunch of messages to one person in quick succession. Spacing out the follow-ups keeps you within the texting rules.

Q: Where do my leads show up in the pipeline during a blast?
A: After the first text sends, the lead gets an opportunity in the Messaged stage of the text blast pipeline. After three texts with no reply, it moves to 3rd Message No Reply. After the final text with no reply, it moves to No SMS Response. If they reply at any point, it moves to Lead Replied.

Q: What are the SMS tags on my contacts for?
A: As a contact moves through a flow, it's tagged with the message it's about to receive (SMS 1, SMS 2, and so on). If a send fails, for example because you ran out of funds or hit your limit, the contact is removed from the flow but keeps that tag, so when you add it back, the blast picks up at the message it was on. Don't change these tags yourself, or the blast won't know where to resume.

Q: A send failed partway through my blast. Do my leads have to start over?
A: No. When a send fails, the contact is removed from the flow but keeps the tag for the message it was on. Add the contact back to the blast and it skips ahead to that message, all the way through to the seventh. This only works if you leave the SMS tags alone, so don't add, remove, or edit them yourself.

Q: Do I need to buy my first phone number?

Q: Should I get a toll-free number?

## 3-3 Importing a List

Q: What kind of phone numbers should be in my list?
A: Only mobile numbers. REIblast is built to text blast, so landlines, VoIP numbers, and desk phones can't receive your texts. Your list should also have DNC (Do Not Call) numbers removed.

Q: Why should I clean my list before uploading it?
A: If you upload a list without scrubbing it, you end up texting numbers that aren't mobile, like landlines, VoIPs, and desk phones. Each of those logs an error on your SMS campaign, and the more errors you get, the faster your campaign gets restricted.

Q: Do I need to scrub my list for DNC?
A: Your list should have no DNC numbers. Most lead providers scrub against the Do Not Call list automatically when you export your leads, but you can also clean your list yourself or use REIscrub, which removes both non-mobile numbers and DNC numbers automatically.

Q: What is REIscrub?
A: REIscrub is a tool inside REItools that cleans your lead list by automatically removing every number that isn't mobile and every number that's on the DNC list. It works with lists from BatchLeads, PropStream, and DealMachine.

Q: How do I scrub my list with REIscrub?
A: Click REItools in the left sidebar of your CRM and log in with the one-time passcode sent to your phone. Launch REIscrub, open BatchLeads Import, add your lead list, and process the file. Review the results, then download the cleaned file as a CSV and give it a name that tells you what it is (for example, "Land Ohio Mobile Scrub").

Q: Can I use REIscrub with PropStream or DealMachine lists?
A: Yes. The import option is called BatchLeads Import because it was originally built for BatchLeads, but it now works with BatchLeads, PropStream, and DealMachine lists.

Q: What does REIscrub show me after processing my list?
A: It shows the total records in your file, how many had mobile numbers, how many duplicates were found, and how many leads are ready to import. It also previews the cleaned data (first name, last name, address, city, state, zip, phone, and email) in the exact format of the file you'll download.

Q: Can REIscrub send my list straight into my CRM?
A: Not yet. For now, download the cleaned CSV from REIscrub and import it into your contacts. A feature to push lists straight to the CRM is planned.

Q: Why can't I get into REItools?
A: If your subscription is canceled, paused, or not active, you won't be able to get into REItools and you'll see an error message. Make sure your account is active.

Q: Is REIscore working?
A: REIscore is currently disabled. REIscrub is working.

Q: How do I import my leads?
A: Go to Contacts and click Import, then Start CSV Import. Choose Contacts as what you're importing, upload your CSV, choose "Create and update contacts," and click Next. Check the field mapping, make sure Property Address is mapped to Street Address, create a Smart List for the import, and start the import.

Q: Should I choose "Create contacts" or "Create and update contacts" when importing?
A: Always choose "Create and update contacts." With create and update, a duplicate lead updates the existing contact, so if that person already opted out, the contact stays marked Do Not Disturb. If you only create contacts, a duplicate becomes a brand new contact without Do Not Disturb, and if you text someone who already opted out, you've broken the law.

Q: My list got smaller after I imported it. Why?
A: When you use "Create and update contacts," duplicate leads update existing contacts instead of creating new ones, so your list may shrink depending on how many duplicates it had, such as leads with the same phone number. That's what keeps you from texting people who already asked not to be texted.

Q: What fields do I need to map when importing?
A: Most fields map automatically: first name, last name, city, state, zip, phone, and email. The one that usually doesn't map is the property address, so choose Street Address for it. If your CSV has extra columns you don't want to import, you can skip them.

Q: Should I add tags or a workflow when importing a list?
A: Not on your first run. Just create a Smart List. As you get more experienced, you can tag contacts or put them straight into the text blast during import.

Q: What is a Smart List and why should I make one when importing?
A: A Smart List is a saved filter that lets you pull up a specific group of contacts right away without filtering again. Create one every time you import a list, and name it so you know what's in it, like "Ohio Vacant Land 10-8."

Q: I started an import but my contacts aren't showing up. Is it broken?
A: The import takes a little time to process. In Contacts, open Bulk Actions (to the right of Smart Lists) to see the import and what percentage has processed, and click its statistics to see how many contacts have been imported so far. When it finishes, you can view the uploaded contacts from Actions or open the Smart List you created.

Q: Where can I see my past imports?
A: When you start a CSV import in Contacts, there's a record of every import you've done in the system.

## 3-4 Blasting a List

Q: How do I send my first blast?
A: Open the Smart List (or imported contacts) you want to text in Contacts and select the contacts. Click Trigger, give the action a name, and choose the Start SMS Blast workflow. Set your sending schedule (a scheduled time or drip mode), then click Add to Automation.

Q: What are the steps to get a blast going?
A: Four steps: get your phone number, clean your list, import it, and add it to the Start SMS Blast automation. REIblast buys your first phone number and assigns it to you when your A2P campaign is set up, so in practice you just get your list, scrub it, import it, and blast it.

Q: Which workflow do I add my contacts to?
A: Always start with the Start SMS Blast workflow. It has no trigger of its own; you add contacts to it directly. It assigns each contact to you as the user (REIblast sets this up), so your texts say who they're from, then splits contacts evenly across SMS Flow 1, 2, and 3.

Q: Should I send to my whole list at once?
A: It works, but it isn't recommended when you're sending thousands of texts. Sending everything at once can overload the system, cause a lot of opt-outs and errors, and keep some messages from sending. Use drip mode to send in batches instead.

Q: What drip settings should I use?
A: The recommendation is 10 messages every 1 minute, which works out to about one message every six seconds. It's a recommendation, not a hard limit: you can go higher, like 20 or 30 per minute, or send everything at once. Just know that overloading the system can lead to a lot of errors.

Q: How do I set up drip mode?
A: When you add contacts to the automation, choose drip mode. Pick when it starts, how many contacts to send per batch, how often to repeat, and which days to run, then click Add to Automation.

Q: Can I schedule a blast for later?
A: Yes. When you add contacts to the automation, you can set a specific time for it to start or use drip mode. Even if you add contacts at midnight, the automation won't text anyone outside its allowed sending hours.

Q: How do I check that my blast is running?
A: Open Bulk Actions in Contacts. Your blast shows up under the name you gave it, with the operation listed as Workflow, its status, and statistics you can click into.

Q: What else can I do with contacts I've selected?
A: Once you select contacts, you can export them, trigger an automation, send an email or mass email, add tags, delete them, send WhatsApp messages, or request reviews.

## 3-5 Working the Pipeline

Q: I sent my blast. Now what do I do?
A: Work your pipeline. Every day, check the Lead Replied stage, respond to each reply quickly, and move each person to where they belong: Interested if they're open to selling, or Dead/Lost Lead if they're not interested or opted out. Most of the pipeline moves on its own; Lead Replied is where your work is.

Q: What are the stages in my pipeline and how do they work?
A: Your Text Blast pipeline has these stages: Messaged, 3rd Message No Reply, Lead Replied, Interested, Offer Made, Contract Signed, No SMS Response, and Dead/Lost Lead. Messaged, 3rd Message No Reply, No SMS Response, and Lead Replied move automatically through the automations. Interested, Offer Made, Contract Signed, and Dead/Lost Lead are yours to manage after you've talked to the lead.

Q: Which leads do I need to move myself?
A: Only leads that have replied. The automations move leads into Messaged, 3rd Message No Reply, No SMS Response, and Lead Replied on their own. After someone replies, you move them to Interested, Offer Made, Contract Signed, or Dead/Lost Lead.

Q: Someone hasn't replied to my first text. Should I follow up?
A: No, there's nothing for you to do. Most people don't answer the first text. They stay in Messaged, and your follow-up messages keep reaching out automatically.

Q: A lead got three texts and never replied. What should I do?
A: Nothing. The automation moves them to 3rd Message No Reply on its own. A lot of people answer on the fourth or fifth message, so let the sequence keep going.

Q: A lead got all seven texts and never replied. Should I keep texting them?
A: No. When the sequence finishes, they land in No SMS Response automatically. Seven texts with no answer is a clear signal, and piling on more texts is how numbers get flagged. You can let them rest, put them on a long-term drip (like one text a month), move them to Dead/Lost Lead, or try a phone call if the property looks like a strong deal, in case you had the wrong number.

Q: Someone replied STOP. What do I do?
A: The opt-out works automatically: STOP or any other opt-out keyword opts them out and turns on Do Not Disturb, so they won't get another text. They still land in Lead Replied like any other reply, so add the tag "Remove from All - Dead Lead" to take them out of all workflows, then move them to Dead/Lost Lead.

Q: Someone said "not interested, stop texting me" but didn't reply with just STOP. What do I do?
A: The automatic opt-out only catches the opt-out keyword, so this one is on you. Turn on Do Not Disturb for their contact so they never get another text, then move them to Dead/Lost Lead. Texting someone who asked you to stop is how you get reported, and reports hurt your number and can get your campaign suspended so you can't blast at all.

Q: A seller said they might sell. What do I do?
A: Respond fast, because interest is highest right after a reply. Answer quickly, try to get them on the phone, and once you've talked and they're open to an offer, move them to Interested. On the call, get the property's condition, their motivation, their price, and their timeline so you can make an offer.

Q: How do I see only the leads that replied?
A: In Contacts, click Filters and select Opportunity Stage. Choose the Text Blast pipeline and the stage you're working (like Lead Replied), then click Apply. Open the first contact and use the arrows at the top to move through each one.

Q: How do I move a lead to a different stage?
A: Open the conversation with the contact. Any opportunity stage change shows up in the conversation with a Details link; click Details, and in the menu that pops up, change the opportunity stage, for example to Interested or Dead/Lost Lead.

Q: Most of my leads ended up in Dead/Lost Lead. Is something wrong?
A: No. The majority of the people you blast will end up in Dead/Lost Lead. That's normal; text blasting works because you can reach thousands of people a day and get a few interested sellers.

Q: How much time do I need to put into working my pipeline?
A: You don't need to spend eight hours a day, but it takes consistent effort. Blasting every day, uploading lists, and working Lead Replied daily will get you a deal faster than treating it as a part-time afterthought.

## 3-7 Troubleshooting

Q: My texts aren't going through. What should I check first?
A: Check two things before anything else, because they cause the large majority of texting problems: Is your A2P registration approved? Does your wallet have money in it? If either one is the issue, nothing else matters yet. If both are good, open the failed message and check its status and error code.

Q: How do I find out why a text failed?
A: Open the contact's conversation and click the message that failed. Failed messages usually show a red triangle; hover over it to see the five-digit error code and sometimes a description. Write the code down, since it points to the fix and it's the first thing support will ask for.

Q: What do the message statuses mean?
A: Pending or Queued means the message is waiting to send; a few moments is normal, but if it's stuck for a long time, check your A2P and wallet. Sent means it reached the carrier but delivery isn't confirmed. Delivered is the only status that means the text landed on the phone. Undelivered means the carrier took it but couldn't deliver it, usually because of a landline, carrier filtering, or a bad number. Failed means it never left your account, usually because of A2P, your wallet, your number, or the person having opted out.

Q: My texts say "Sent." Does that mean they were delivered?
A: No. Sent only means the message reached the carrier. Delivered is the only status that confirms the text landed. If you're seeing a lot of Sent but not Delivered, that's usually carrier filtering.

Q: What does error 21610 mean?
A: The person opted out by replying STOP or another opt-out keyword, or they're marked Do Not Disturb. Don't try to resend; remove them from your active lists. If they text START, Do Not Disturb is turned off and you can text them again.

Q: What does error 21635 or 21661 mean?
A: You're texting a landline, or the number you're sending from isn't set up to text. Remove landlines from your list and double-check that your own number is SMS capable. The number REIblast sets up for you during A2P is always SMS capable; numbers you buy yourself need SMS capability selected when you buy them.

Q: What does error 30003 mean?
A: The recipient's phone was off, in airplane mode, or had no signal. It's usually temporary, so it's fine to try again later. If you see it across a lot of contacts at once, it's more likely filtering than bad luck.

Q: What does error 30005 mean?
A: The number is inactive or doesn't exist. Remove it, and clean your list before importing. Skip-traced data is never 100% accurate, so a few of these are normal, but if you see it across a lot of contacts, it's a data quality problem.

Q: What does error 30006 mean?
A: It's another landline code, or the recipient's carrier can't receive texts. Remove landlines and clean your list before you import.

Q: What does error 30007 mean?
A: The carrier filtered your message because it looked like spam. This is the most important error to understand. Rewrite your message so it sounds personal and plain, like a real person wrote it: remove shortened links, avoid spammy words, all caps, lots of exclamation points, and emojis, vary the wording across your list, and always say who you are and include opt-out language.

Q: What does error 30008 mean?
A: It's an unknown error. Try again. If it keeps happening, collect a few examples and contact support.

Q: What does error 30032 or 30034 mean?
A: Your number's registration isn't complete, which means your A2P isn't approved yet. Nothing will send until it is.

Q: My blast isn't sending or is stuck on pending. What's wrong?
A: It's almost always one of four things, in this order: your A2P isn't approved yet, so check your registration status; your wallet is empty, so add funds; you don't have an SMS-capable, registered number; or you've hit your daily ramp-up limit, in which case wait until tomorrow or split your blast across a few days. Checking the first two clears up most cases.

Q: My texts show sent but nobody is getting them. Why?
A: If you're seeing Sent or Undelivered with error 30007, the carriers are filtering your messages as spam. Common triggers are links (especially shortened links like bit.ly), spammy words, all caps, lots of exclamation points or emojis, sending the exact same message to a big list, or a number with complaints against it. Rewrite your message to sound personal, remove shortened links, vary the wording, and always include who you are and your opt-out language.

Q: I got error 30007 on a text I sent manually. Why?
A: Error 30007 doesn't only apply to blasts. If a message you sent by hand gets filtered, its wording contained language the carriers didn't approve of. Rewrite it so it's A2P compliant and send it again.

Q: My blast started sending and then stopped partway through. Why?
A: You hit a ceiling, and there are only two reasons. Either your wallet ran out of money partway through and couldn't recharge (for example, because your card on file expired, was declined, or didn't have enough money), or your number hit its daily ramp-up limit. Check your wallet first; if it's empty, fix your card or add funds and the rest can go out. If your wallet had money, it's the ramp-up limit, and sending picks back up tomorrow.

Q: How do I avoid hitting my daily limit in the middle of a blast?
A: While your number is still ramping up, split big blasts across a few days instead of sending everything at once.

Q: Some contacts got my text and others didn't. Is something wrong?
A: No, partial delivery is normal on every list and is almost always about the numbers, not your account. Landlines can't receive texts, invalid or inactive numbers fail, and anyone who's opted out or on Do Not Disturb gets skipped. Clean your list before you blast, and check a few failed messages: codes like 21610, 30005, or 30006 mean it's the numbers.

Q: My blast automation is grayed out or won't run. Is it broken?
A: No. Sending features stay locked until your A2P registration is approved. That's the carriers, not something REIblast can switch on early. Once your A2P is approved, your automations unlock on their own with nothing for you to do.

Q: My wallet won't recharge or a charge failed. What do I do?
A: A failed charge means your card was declined, for any reason, such as being expired, locked, short on funds, or flagged by your bank. Since everything you send draws from your wallet, a failed charge stops your texts, and after three failed recharges your account is paused. Update or unlock your card in Settings, then Billing, then Payments, add funds to your wallet manually, and make sure auto recharge has a low balance limit and recharge amount set.

Q: My number was working and then stopped. What happened?
A: It's usually been flagged or suspended, almost always after spam complaints or a run of 30007 errors. Look at what you've been sending and how many people are opting out. A badly burned number is sometimes better replaced than saved. If you think your number has been suspended, contact support.

Q: My import failed or some of my contacts are missing. What's wrong?
A: It's almost always the file, not the account. Common causes are wrong or missing column headers, inconsistently formatted phone numbers, rows missing a phone number or other required field (which get skipped), or contacts that came in marked Do Not Disturb. Start from a clean file with clear headers and one contact per row, format every phone number the same way (ideally +1 followed by the 10 digits), and after importing, compare the number of contacts that came in against your file. If they don't match, fix the skipped rows and re-import just those.

Q: My import still hasn't shown up. What should I do?
A: Give it a few minutes and check Bulk Actions in Contacts to see its progress, since large lists (like 5,000 contacts) take time. If it still hasn't uploaded after an hour, contact support. Lists cleaned with REIscrub are already formatted to import without issues.

Q: Where do I see replies to my texts? I can't find them.
A: Every reply lands in that contact's conversation in Conversations, not in the blast tool or the automation. In Conversations, new replies show as Unread, All shows everything, and Recent sorts by most recent. Anyone who replies also moves to Lead Replied in your pipeline. If you're not getting pinged, turn on your notifications so you know the moment someone answers.

Q: How do I keep my number from getting flagged or shut down?
A: Follow the texting rules: respect the ramp-up period and don't blast a new number at full volume, always say who you are and include opt-out language, text people who'd expect to hear from you or have real motivation, remove cold leads who never respond, and keep messages personal and varied instead of one identical mass blast. Carriers suspend numbers that show lots of STOP replies, spam complaints, or too much volume too fast.

Q: My number got flagged. What do I do?
A: Stop sending, figure out what triggered it, and contact support. A badly burned number may need to be replaced rather than saved.

Q: When should I contact support?
A: Contact support if your A2P shows approved and messages still fail, if the same error keeps happening across many contacts even after cleaning your list and rewriting your message, if your number was suspended and needs to be reviewed or replaced, or if a payment problem won't clear after you've updated your card.

Q: What should I send support so they can fix my issue fast?
A: Send three things up front: the error code and message status, at least three example messages from the last 24 hours (with which contacts and what time), and what you've already tried. That usually lets support fix it in one round.

Q: Can I book a support call for a texting problem?
A: Yes, using the booking link in the Support Calendar channel in the Discord, but make sure you've done the troubleshooting first: check that your A2P is approved, check that your wallet is funded, and look up the error code on the failed message. Support calls are for legitimate blockers that troubleshooting can't fix. If you book a call without having done the troubleshooting yourself, the call will be cancelled.
